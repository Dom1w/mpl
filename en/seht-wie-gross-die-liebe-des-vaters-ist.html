See How Great the Father’s Love Is!

From the revelations of the Heavenly Father to Mother Eugenia E. Ravasio

This brochure of the Marian Movement of Priests and Laity gathers the revelations of the Heavenly Father to Mother Eugenia Elisabetta Ravasio (1907–1990): His great longing to be recognized, honored and loved by all people as a loving Father. It also contains testimonies of the Church’s examination of this message, as well as prayers and an Our Father novena to the Heavenly Father.

On the Way to the Father’s Heart

Whether we realize it or not, we are all on the way to the Father’s heart. And St. Augustine speaks from our soul when, on his path of conversion, he prays: Our heart is restless until it rests in You, O God!

When we look at the colorful and varied life stories of the saints, we meet some who found their place in the heart of the Heavenly Father from childhood, and others who discovered the loving fatherly heart and the true faith only after many wrong turns and detours, such as St. Paul, for example. And many a human being who walks over this earth, strewn with thistles and thorns, finds his way home to the saving anchor of the divine Father’s heart only in his last hour of life.

Now the decisive question arises: how do we find the way to the Father’s heart, or rather, how does the Father find the way into our heart?

Jesus gives us a very clear answer in the Gospel, one that every child can understand: No one comes to the Father but by Me! (Jn 14,6)

And the Savior goes on to explain: If a man loves Me, he will keep My word, and My Father will love him, and We will come to him and make Our home with him. (Jn 14,23)

By this Jesus wants to tell us that God Himself sets out on the way to us when we long for Him with our whole heart, love Him, and unite our will with His.

In this way we build Him, as it were, a golden ladder to heaven, by which He can descend into our poor human heart, in order to set up there the throne of His glory.

For the glory of God is a living man, says St. Irenaeus.

The most beautiful model on this path of union with God is the most blessed Virgin Mary, who through her great love and longing drew the Redeemer into her virginal womb.

At the same time, the Mother of God is also the great mediatrix of grace, who leads us to her beloved Son. For through Mary we come to Jesus, and through Jesus, who made Himself a bridge for us, we come to the Heavenly Father.1

1 Because the world has brought forth thorns and suffering through sin, and this river flows on like a stormy sea, I have given you the bridge so that you do not drown. (…) But that My Son has made Himself a bridge for you is not enough to attain life – you must also follow this path across the bridge. (From: Der Dialog, Caterina von Siena, Ein Gespräch mit Gott über Seine Vorsehung, Kleinhain 2017, pp. 52–53, 100)

The Practice of the Heavenly Father

When a school class was asked the question: Where does the dear God live?, a little girl, the daughter of a doctor, answered bravely, thinking of her own father: The dear God, our Heavenly Father, lives in heaven, but He has His practice on earth. This little girl truly hit the nail on the head, for since the Fall our earth has been transformed into a huge hospital.

How comforting it is to know that among all the “patients” infected by original sin, the divine physician Himself lives and works, to heal His badly wounded children with the most select and precious “treatments” for eternal life, and to bring them home. With everything this earthly life gives us of beauty and joy, but also tests us with hardship, the divine chief physician applies His special therapies, and through His kind providence, which embraces us lovingly at every moment, offers us the best means of healing and sanctification for body and soul.

The most important institution in the great hospital of this world is the holy Catholic Church, which our Lord Jesus Christ Himself founded and to which He entrusted all His redeeming merits.

She is like a new Garden of Eden, in which the bubbling spring of the holy sacraments pours out over the whole world in a sevenfold stream of grace. Those who draw eagerly from this divine spring receive abundant graces to meet the challenges of daily life well, and also to overcome victoriously the temptations of the devil, whose unbridled hatred and envy cannot bear the love between God and man.2

With tender shepherd’s care, God the Father once spoke to St. Catherine of Siena:

2 Cf. 1 Pet 5,8-9.

3 Der Dialog, p. 359.

God wants to lead souls to holiness. For this reason He lets human society continue to exist, despite the worm that gnaws at it through so many souls who blaspheme His name and disregard His commandments. In His kind providence He turns even the evil that happens in the world to good for all who love Him and trust in Him. In the whole world He allows no event and tolerates no evil without its contributing to the progress of souls.

God takes care of His magnificently built world, and without effort He could build a thousand other, mightier and more beautiful ones. But what occupies His thoughts far more, what above all touches His heart and joyfully moves heaven, are the souls: innocence preserved or regained, an act of love, a soul’s resolve to give itself wholly to the Lord, a sigh of longing for His heart, a prayer. When this longing of the Heavenly Father finds a loving answer in our heart, and the thirst for His honor and for the salvation of souls gives our life ever more meaning and purpose, then we have the most beautiful and deepest reason for true joy. For in this attitude we walk, as it were, in the footsteps of Jesus, for whom the honor of the Father and the salvation of souls were the pulse of His whole divine-human life.4

4 Cf. Jn 4,32-34: My food is to do the will of Him who sent Me. The will of the Father, however, is the salvation of men, the rescue of the world. The Little Tree of the Heavenly Father

To remind us anew of the great truths of the Catholic faith, in 1932 the Heavenly Father gave us a very special message through Mother Eugenia Elisabetta Ravasio (1907–1990), to whom He communicated His great longing to be recognized, honored and loved by all people.

But who was Mother Eugenia, whom the Heavenly Father lovingly used to call “My favored daughter, My little tree”?

Mother Eugenia, who came from northern Italy, was a missionary sister in the congregation of Our Lady of the Apostles, and lived in heroic devotion for the unity of all people as children of God in Christ and to the honor of the Heavenly Father.

With this intention she founded in Rome the “Unitas in Christo ad Patrem”, which to this day carries on her charism.

As Superior General she opened numerous novitiates, centers with chapels, schools and hospital wards in Europe. She discovered the first medicine against leprosy and realized the idea of a city for leprosy patients, which she had built in Azopté (Ivory Coast) between 1938 and 1941.

On July 1 and August 12, 1932, she received the message of the Father, who appeared to her in visible form to entrust her with the mission of preparing people to take the Father into their hearts, the Father who wants to establish His kingdom of love in them and with them.

The Heavenly Father and Christ, Alpha and Omega (book illumination)

The Grain of Wheat That Falls into the Earth

In 1947 Mother Eugenia was elected Superior General for a third term, by way of an exceptional dispensation, on account of her extraordinary love and remarkable gift of leadership. But God allowed a sister from her own ranks to accuse and slander her before the Holy Office. Thereupon the gifted superior had to resign from her office. She was forbidden to speak about the message, and the pictures of the Divine Father had to be removed from all the mission houses. Mother Eugenia recognized inwardly that she would also soon be asked to leave her institute, which in fact happened in 1956, after a long series of slanders and accusations. At the same time, however, God also let her understand that she would found a new congregation. In the following years Mother Eugenia struggled for clarity about how to fulfill this mission. There were several years of inner darkness and much outward suffering. Only in 1980 did she receive the ecclesiastical permission to found the missionaries “Unitas in Christo ad Patrem”. And in 1989 the Vicar General of Vatican City State, Petrus Canisius van Lierde OSB, gave the imprimatur for the message.

Mother Eugenia had given God everything, and so He also gave her everything for which she asked Him. For this reason many people turned to her in trust in her power of intercession. One day a paralyzed girl also came to her. Mother Eugenia promised her: Be at peace, I will take your suffering upon myself. You are still young and must still do much good. A short time later several people witnessed how the sick girl stood up and could walk without any help. Mother Eugenia, by contrast, spent the last years of her life in a wheelchair. Thus she gave her life as an atoning sacrifice unreservedly into the Father’s hands, so that what He had already let her recognize in 1947 might be fulfilled as soon as possible:

On August 10, 1990, the prophetess of the Divine Father died at the age of 83, with a reputation for holiness.

5 Mother Eugenia speaks here of suffering offered up in love, which bears rich fruit when it is united with the sufferings of the Redeemer.

The Church’s Examination

When Pope Pius XII heard of Mother Eugenia, he sent Fr. Girard Matthieu, a priest he trusted, to her, to obtain information first-hand. While the priest was waiting for Mother Eugenia, he saw her coming toward him from the end of the corridor: floating half a meter above the ground and surrounded by a radiant light that emanated from her. God worked this wonder as proof of the authenticity of His revelations to this chosen soul.

After a ten-year period of examination under the direction of Bishop Alexandre Caillot (1861–1957), the message of the Heavenly Father, which Mother Eugenia received in Latin, was declared authentic.6

6 This very experienced and zealous shepherd of souls had set up a commission to investigate the message and its recipient. For ten years several theologians worked on this task, concerned not only with the content of the message, but above all with the fact that the Divine Father showed Himself visibly as a person to Mother Eugenia. In January 1934 the theologians found in Thomas Aquinas the answer to their own objections. The answer of the great Doctor of the Church to the distinction between “appearance” and “mission” was convincing. It overcame the hurdle that paralyzed the entire investigation. The fact that the Divine Father gave the message in Latin, although Mother Eugenia, because of her only three years of schooling, did not speak Latin, was a further strong proof of authenticity for Bishop Alexandre Caillot.

The Father Speaks to His Children

The Reason for His Coming

  • I lay down My crown and all My glory, in order to take on the humble attitude of an ordinary human being!
  • I come to finally drive out the excessive fear that My creatures have of Me. I want to make it clear to them that it is My joy to be known and loved by My sons and daughters, that is, by all of present and future humanity.
  • My presence among you is like the sun in the earthly world. If you are willing to receive Me, I will come very close to you. I will take up My dwelling in you, enlighten you and warm you through My infinite love.
  • Oh, how My fatherly love has been forgotten by people! And yet I love you with such great tenderness.
  • If you love Me and call Me Father with trust, using this lovable name, you already begin here on earth to live that love and that trust which will make up your entire happiness in eternity.

The Holy Family with the Lamb (painting by Raphael)

The Father of Families

  • If families honor Me and love Me as their Father, I will send them peace and, with it, My providence.
  • How great would be My joy if I could see parents teach their children to call upon Me often by My name, to call Me Father, for that is what I truly am. How much I long to see trust and love for Me arise in these young souls, the love of children for their Father! I have done everything for you; will you now do this for Me?
  • I want to dwell in every family, as in My house, so that everyone can say with the greatest certainty: We have a Father who is infinitely good, infinitely rich and unimaginably merciful. He thinks of us and is near us, He watches over us, He Himself protects us, and He will give us everything we lack when we ask Him for it. All His riches belong to us, we will have everything we need.

The Heavenly Father (painting)

The Father of All People

  • If anyone loves Me and entrusts himself to Me, I will send him a ray of peace in all his needs, all his confusion and suffering and every sorrow, especially when he calls upon Me and loves Me as his Father.
  • If workers, industrialists and the various other craftsmen call upon Me and honor Me, I will give them My peace and My strength and show Myself to them as a good and merciful Father.

The Greatest Good Intention

7 When you pray these words, you awaken the good intention in the most perfect way. Also, people who pray in this way take part in the communion of saints, or rather, each time they use these words they enter, as it were anew and even more closely, into the communion of saints – not only of the saints in heaven, but of all members of the triple Church. Therefore no one can begin the new day with God in a more excellent way. (From: Aszese und Mystik, by Prof. Albert Drexel, Jestetten 1977)

  • If I am called upon and honored in every Christian community, I will give My peace and show Myself as the most loving Father, and by My power I will assure eternal salvation to souls.
  • If all humanity calls upon Me and honors Me, I will send it the spirit of peace, which will settle upon it like blessed dew.

The return of the prodigal son to the merciful father

The Merciful Father

  • If all nations call upon Me and honor Me in this way, there will be no more discord and no more wars, for I am the God of peace, and where I am, there will be no war.
  • Come close to My spring, which always flows from the depths of My fatherly heart (…) and plunge into the ocean of My love, so as to live only in Me. If you want to drink from this spring, then fathom Me, learn, so that you may come to know Me; and as soon as you know Me, plunge into the ocean of My love by entrusting yourselves to Me with a trust that transforms you and that I cannot resist, so that I will forgive you your faults and fill you with the greatest graces.8

8 Mother Eugenia notes on this: Our Father said to me in a private conversation: “The spring stands as a symbol of My being known, and the ocean stands for My love and your trust.”

I have come to let you taste true freedom, the freedom from which true happiness springs: all the joys of this earth are nothing compared to this happiness.

You, My children, who are outside the Catholic Church, you should know that My fatherly love does not exclude you. I address you with My tender call, for you too are My children. Even if up to today you have succumbed to the allurements with which the devil enticed you, recognize that he has deceived you; come to Me, your Father, and I will receive you with joy and love!

And you, My children, who live in darkness because you have lost your faith: lift up your eyes, and you will see bright rays that will light you up. I am the sun that shines upon you, that is warm and warms you. See, and you will recognize that I am your Creator, your Father, your only God.

Come, come to Me with trust. (…) Your sins are to Me like iron, your deeds of love like gold. (…) This means that with a little love, infinitely great transgressions are wiped out.

All who call Me Father from the heart, even if only once, will not be lost; no, eternal life in the community of the chosen is assured to them.

The Father takes His sacrificed Son into His arms (Throne of Grace)

The Ways of the Father

I come to you in two ways: through the Cross and the Eucharist.

The Cross is My way to descend to My children, for through the Cross I have redeemed you through My Son. For you the Cross is the way to ascend to My Son, and through My Son to Me. (…)

In the Eucharist I dwell among you like a father in his family. I wanted My Son to institute the Eucharist, so as to make out of every tabernacle a vessel of My graces, My riches and My love, so that I may distribute them to people, My children.

Through these two ways I let My power and My infinite mercy flow unceasingly to people.

I can understand the weakness of My children. Therefore I asked My Son to give them means to rise again after their falls into sin. These means will help them to cleanse themselves from sin, so that they become children of My love once more. These means are principally the seven sacraments, and a very significant, important means is above all the Cross, for it will save you despite your falls; it is the Blood of My Son, which pours over you every moment, provided you accept it, both through the sacrament of Penance and through the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass.9

9 The celebration of the holy Mass is the source and summit of the whole Christian life, the highest worship, the mysterious re-presentation of the sacrifice of the Cross of Jesus Christ. In the obedient self-surrender of Jesus all glory and honor is given to the Father. At the same time, Christ’s love opens for every person a way that leads to the heart of the Father. Our whole being, our human weakness, our cares and needs, as well as the guilt of all people, we lay into the event of the consecration in the holy Mass. For in the broken body and the poured-out blood of Christ we find healing and atonement for the sins of the whole world. The gifted Steyl missionary Brother Kostka Wasel (1868–1946) was privileged to experience the mystery of the holy sacrifice of the Mass in visions for over 40 years. He describes especially movingly how, at the moment of consecration, the Heavenly Father takes His sacrificed Son into His arms. In His beloved Son, however, the Father also takes us, His children, into His arms, and we may be sure that this is the greatest and most sublime moment to ask our Heavenly Father for every grace and for the deep personal healing of all our wounds, for now the throne of grace stands open to all who implore help with trust. (Cf. Das heilige Messopfer in Schauungen erlebt, ed. by Klemens Kiser, Lauerz 1996, pp. 69–128)

My children, I will not express the whole greatness of My infinite love, for it is enough for you to open Holy Scripture and to contemplate the Cross, the tabernacle and the Blessed Sacrament of the altar, so that you can understand to what point I have loved you!

The Heavenly Father, surrounded by saints (fresco by Perugino)

A Feast for the Heavenly Father

A day, or at least a Sunday, shall be dedicated to Me, on which I am honored in a very special way under the name Father of all humanity.10 For this feast I wish to have its own Mass formulary and its own proper for the Liturgy of the Hours. It is not difficult to find the texts for it in Holy Scripture. If you prefer to show Me this special veneration on a Sunday, I choose the first Sunday in August; if you take a weekday, I always prefer August 7.

10 The object of the mission entrusted to Mother Eugenia is very precise and, from the doctrinal point of view, also, as it seems to me, legitimate and appropriate. The precise mission is: to make the Father known and honored, above all the demand to the Church for the introduction of a special feast. The investigation has shown that a liturgical feast in honor of the Father would fit quite well with the whole line of Catholic worship and agree with the traditional movement of Catholic prayer, the ascent to the Father through the Son in the Holy Spirit, as the eucharistic prayers of the holy Mass and the liturgical offerings to the Father in the holy sacrifice demonstrate. On the other hand, it is strange that so far there is no special feast in honor of the Father: the Trinity is honored as such, the Word and the Holy Spirit are honored in their mission and in their outward manifestations, only the Father has no feast of His own that would draw the attention of the Christian people in a special way to His person. (Bishop Alexandre Caillot)

The whole clergy should work to bring this veneration to flower; above all, the clerics should make Me known to people as the One I am and who I will always be for them: namely the most tender and most lovable of all fathers.

If all those people who are far from our Catholic Church were to hear of this Father, who loves them and who is their Creator and God, if you were to tell them of this Father, who desires to give them eternal life, then a large part of these people would come to this Father, even the most stubborn among them.

The Image of the Heavenly Father

I wish that the clergy would see to it that I am admitted in all families and in the hospitals, also in the workshops and factories, in the barracks and in the assembly halls in which the ministers of the nations make their decisions, indeed finally everywhere where My creatures are present, even if only a single one of them is there.

The visible sign of My invisible presence shall be an image that shows that I am truly here and present. (…) In this way all My children will live, as it were, under the gaze of their tender Father. Undoubtedly I am also everywhere now, but I want to be represented in a way that is perceptible to the senses.

I wish especially to be honored in the seminaries, the novitiates, in the schools and homes. All, from the smallest to the greatest, shall be able to recognize Me and love Me as their Father, their Creator and their Redeemer.11

Lord, show us the Father!

St. Hilary of Poitiers says that the great work of Christ was to make the Father known. In the Sermon on the Mount Jesus speaks of the Father 17 times, in the whole Gospel of Matthew 47 times, in John 115 times, of which 43 are in the farewell discourses.

In the service of this great work of Christ also stands the mission that Mother Eugenia received from the Heavenly Father Himself: to tell people how great the Father’s longing is to be recognized, loved and honored by all His creatures.

Jesus is one in being with the Father, whose goodness and love He reflects in the most perfect way.

If we want to know whether our image of the Father is moving in a good direction, we need only ask ourselves whether, in the life and actions of Jesus, we recognize the merciful love of the Heavenly Father.

Even the disciples of Jesus had to win the truth about the nature of the Father step by step, which finds expression not least in the longing request of the holy Apostle Philip: Lord, show us the Father! And Jesus answers him: Philip, he who has seen Me has seen the Father. (Jn 14,8-9)

And when the disciples saw their Master praying, there arose in them the heartfelt wish to pray likewise to the Father. From this longing comes their request: Lord, teach us to pray! (Lk 11,1)

And Jesus leads them into the great Lord’s Prayer, which to this very hour rises from the hearts of countless Christians to the heart of the Father.

In the footsteps of the apostles and the first Christians, who heard the sound of the voice of Jesus and saw His facial expression and bearing when He prayed the Our Father, we may rediscover anew what a tender, great and sublime Father we have.

In this sense St. John Cassian (†435) says: The Our Father is a lifting up to God alone, a great fire of love. The soul melts in it, sinks into holy love and converses with God as with its own father, very intimately, in a very special, tender filial love.

When the little St. Thérèse of the Child Jesus was one day completely absorbed in needlework, her sister Céline came and asked: What are you thinking about? And she answered: I am meditating on the Our Father. It is so beautiful to call the dear God Father! And tears shimmered in her eyes.

This intimate relationship of St. Thérèse with her Heavenly Father is also shown in a letter to her sister Agnes, in which she writes: God is more tender than a mother!

And in a letter to Mother Marie de Gonzague she writes: Sometimes when my mind is in such a great aridity that it is impossible to draw forth one single thought to unite me with God, I very slowly recite an “Our Father” and then the angelic salutation; then these prayers give me great delight; they nourish my soul much more than if I had recited them precipitately a hundred times.

We may be aware that when we pray the Our Father reverently, we take refuge in the arms of the Heavenly Father, who protects us with His all-embracing fatherly love and authority from the attacks of evil, as we ask Him at the end of the Our Father: … deliver us from evil. Thus the precious prayer of the Lord also has an outstanding exorcistic effect, as has been confirmed by many priests active in the ministry of liberation and by numerous Christians.12

Mary, Queen of Unity

The Fatherhood of St. Joseph

When the Heavenly Father sent His Son into the world to save us and to make us His children, He chose St. Joseph to represent His divine fatherhood on earth. The perfect trust that Jesus showed His foster father is a shining example for us all. Jesus loved, obeyed and imitated His earthly father, and each time Jesus saw St. Joseph, heard him speak, saw him work or experienced his chaste love for Mary, the humanity of Jesus experienced a perfect mirror image of the Father in heaven.

We too may place all our trust in him and regard ourselves as his children. If Jesus needed St. Joseph according to the plans of the Father, how much more do we need him, this great patron of the holy Church. If our fathers of families take St. Joseph as their model, then this great saint will help them to live their fatherhood entirely according to the heart of the Heavenly Father.

In this way they lay for their children a supporting foundation of life, which becomes a source of divine blessing for their future.13

Prayer and Praise

Our prayer possesses an outstanding value and a power which far surpasses all physical forces taken together. Corporeal beings and created spirits, together with all their works, remain light-years behind the power and worth of a single act of love for God and do not outweigh it. But prayer has the power to obtain for us the grace with which we perform this act of love. Therefore Christian prayer has a greater meaning and scope than the most astonishing discoveries and the greatest talents. It is the great power on earth. So we must learn to think about our prayer in this way.

Descent from the Cross

Give Thanks to the Father with Joy!

When St. Paul calls us to always give thanks to the Heavenly Father, he does so because he knows that sincere thanks is most pleasing to God and at the same time draws countless new spiritual and bodily blessings upon the grateful soul. Joy and contentment go together with the gratitude for all that the Heavenly Father gives us day by day. Gratitude, joy and contentment are, as it were, “sisters” who gladly walk together through this life and find favor with God and with people. Even a person who is weighed down by sin and worry cannot resist these three “sisters” for long when he meets them on his way, for these “three” are “contagious”.

It is still relatively easy to give thanks for everything joyful and beautiful, but what about our thanks when God tests us through cross and suffering, and our prayer requests seemingly go unanswered?

To St. Mariam Baouardy, who was afflicted by countless sufferings and persecutions, the Mother of God once said: Mariam, always be content with your lot, even when you must suffer. The good God will always send you what is necessary.

St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort says: Who could ever explain, who could grasp, what eternal treasure of glory a single moment of a cross willingly borne works in us?

The cross is spared no one in this world, for the cross is the bridge that leads us over the abyss of this world to heaven.

Through the cross God wants to draw to Himself those who do not love Him, to draw closer to Himself those who do not love Him enough, and to unite with Himself those who love Him wholly.

If we also follow the counsel of the Mother of God to St. Mariam and always remain content, saying our grateful “Fiat” to the Divine Will and to all His permissions, then God will lead all situations of our life to good. (Cf. Rom 8,28)

We cannot imagine what great joy and honor we give to the Heavenly Father when, with childlike trust, we entrust our life, prayer and sacrifices to His kind fatherly care.

When St. Gertrude once asked Jesus why He does not grant all prayers, He gave her the following answer: Beloved daughter, when I sometimes do not grant your prayers and wishes, it happens only so that I may give you even more at the right time. In your human weakness you cannot recognize what is most advantageous for you.

If in our human short-sightedness we ask God for a “scorpion,” He will certainly not fulfill this wish, for He will not give us anything that would be to our harm. But, as the Catholic faith teaches, we may be quite certain that every, even the smallest, prayer is carried by our guardian angel to the throne of God and, transformed into a rain of grace, will fall back upon us and upon all for whom we pray.
