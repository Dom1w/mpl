His Name Is Written on Their Foreheads

The Power of the Most Holy Name of Jesus in the Final Battle

This brochure of the Marian Movement of Priests and Laity presents the power of the Most Holy Name of Jesus: the testimonies of numerous saints, the invocation and adoration of this Name in daily life, its healing and liberating power, and its significance in the final battle that the Book of Revelation of St. John describes.

The Annunciation of the Archangel Gabriel to Mary

Foreword

The name Jesus is the name by which God Himself, the second Divine Person of the Most Holy Trinity, willed to be called in His human nature. This name was brought to us from heaven, for it first rang out on earth from the mouth of the Archangel Gabriel. This name is the expression of the meaning and purpose of the Incarnation, namely redemption, for Jesus means Savior in Hebrew. This name was then also written on the wood of the Cross on Golgotha, and since then it adorns all crosses and depictions of the Cross.

Redemption means participation in the divine nature through the redeeming grace of Christ. It is happiness in God already here on earth in the life of grace, and one day in the blessed vision of God. In the Book of the Prophet Habakkuk this beautiful statement stands in the Latin Vulgate version: Exultabo in Deo Iesu meo, (Hab 3,18), that is, I rejoice in God, my Jesus (= Savior). Our eternal happiness will consist in rejoicing in Jesus, our God and Savior, that is, through Him in the Triune God.

We will receive a new name in heaven (cf. Rev 2,17), and on the foreheads of the saved the name of God will be written (cf. Rev 3,12), namely the name of Jesus and the name of the Father. (Cf. Rev 14,1) Already in the Old Covenant God promised us: Every one who is called by My name, whom I created for My glory, whom I formed and made. (Is 43,7) This name, in which God formed the chosen, that is, those who in the end are saved, is the name of our Redeemer. St. Paul teaches that the new man is renewed after the image of the One who created him. (Cf. Col 3,10)

The mission of the Church consists in bearing the redemption of Christ, and thus His name, to the ends of the earth, as the Lord said of the newly converted Paul: He shall carry My name before the Gentiles and kings and the children of Israel. (Acts 9,15) This is indeed the will of God, that all believe in the name of His Son. (Cf. 1 Jn 3,23) For there is no other name given to men by which they can be saved, (Acts 4,12) and whoever calls upon the name of the Lord Jesus Christ will be saved. (Cf. Acts 2,21)

The task of Christians is to glorify God in the name “Christian.” (Cf. 1 Pet 4,16) From the early days of the Church, Clement of Alexandria (early 3rd century) hands down this moving statement about the dignity of Christians:

But the task of the Christian is also to confess the name of Jesus before men (cf. Mt 10,32) and to desire that every tongue confess that Jesus is Lord. (Phil 2,11) For the sake of this confession the Church and true Christians are hated by the world, as the Lord Himself told us: You will be hated by all for My name’s sake; but he who endures to the end will be saved. (Mk 13,13) Yet it should be a joy for a true Christian to suffer dishonor for the name of Jesus. (Acts 5,41)

Every true Christian should be guided in all that he does by this word of the Psalm: Not to us, O LORD, not to us, but to Thy name give glory. (Ps 115,1) If with the help of God we think and act in this way, God will grant us the grace that, when we leave this earthly life, we will have the unspeakable joy of pronouncing with our heart and mouth the most holy, most powerful and sweetest of all names, the name Jesus, or, with St. Philip Neri, cry out: My Jesus! Jesus, be Jesus to me!

January 25, 2021, on the feast of the Conversion of the Apostle Paul
+ Athanasius Schneider, Auxiliary Bishop of the Archdiocese of Saint Mary in Astana

The Holy Family: Mary, Joseph and the Child Jesus

Witnesses to the Power of the Most Holy Name of Jesus

Since the beginnings of the Church, the unfathomable power and wisdom hidden in the Most Holy Name of Jesus have been recognized as wonderful and unique. Into this mystery of the Divine Name the Mother of God, Mary, and St. Joseph have certainly penetrated most deeply. For God entrusted to them His only-begotten Son, in order to convey to all mankind streams of graces from the heart of the Redeemer. Therefore we may with childlike trust ask for the gift of love for the Most Holy Name of Jesus from their hands. For Mary and Joseph are our surest guides on the way to the unceasing prayer of the heart.

St. Ignatius liked very much to pronounce the name Jesus. Of him the following is told: When the Apostles were quarreling among themselves about who among them would be the greatest in the kingdom of heaven, Jesus came with them to Capernaum, called there a four-year-old boy who was standing with his mother in the doorway of the house, took him by the hand and said to the Apostles: Unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven. Whoever humbles himself like this child, he is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven. (Mt 18,3ff)

In later years this boy Ignatius very often went to the place where the Savior had called him, kissed the ground there and said to his companions: Behold, here in this place Jesus took me into His arms.

Ignatius grew up, later became a priest and then bishop in the great city of Antioch, where St. Peter had worked for seven years before. Antioch soon became Christian. When the Roman Emperor Trajan came to Antioch on his military campaign and found the pagan temples empty, he asked the governor who was to blame for the temples standing empty. The governor said: The bishop of the Christians, Ignatius, is to blame. Then the emperor had the aged bishop brought before him and asked him: Are you the evil spirit who stirs up such great unrest in this city? Ignatius answered: He is no evil spirit who carries God in his heart! The emperor said: Do you mean perhaps Jesus of Nazareth? The bishop affirmed this and instructed the emperor about the folly of pagan idolatry. This made the emperor furious, and he said to the soldiers: Ad leonem, that is, to the lions. The Christians in Rome wanted to free him and prevent his death. But Ignatius wrote them a letter and asked them not to snatch the martyr’s crown from him. Arriving in Rome, he was delivered in the amphitheater, before an enormous crowd, to two hungry lions.

St. Ignatius of Antioch

Beforehand St. Ignatius offered a loud prayer and said: The name of Jesus cannot depart from my mouth; and even if it did, it cannot be blotted out of my heart. All the pagan spectators marveled at the steadfastness and the joyful death of this old man. The lions tore him apart and left only a few bones and the heart. In the heart the name of Jesus could be read, formed by blue veins. The companions of St. Ignatius took his relics from the amphitheater at night and carried them to Antioch, where they were kept on the altar and held in high honor. This happened in the year 107. God thus honored this saint, because he had held the name of Jesus in high honor.1

St. Gregory of Tours (538–594) was advised by his guardian angel to write the name of Jesus on a small card and place it under the pillow of his sick father, whereupon the latter quickly recovered.

St. Leonard of Port Maurice (1676–1751) recommended placing a card with the name of Jesus on doors, whereby many were preserved from illness and misfortune of every kind.

1 Source: Spirago 796, op. cit.

To St. Edmund, King of England (840–870), later Archbishop of Canterbury, Jesus Himself taught the power of His Most Holy Name. When Edmund was once traveling alone, a wonderfully beautiful boy appeared to him and asked him: Edmund, do you not know Me? Edmund said no. The boy then said to him: Look at Me and you will recognize who I am. Edmund looked closely at the child and then saw on the forehead the shining letters INRI Iesus Nazarenus Rex Iudaeorum (Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews). Then the child said: Now you know who I am. You see My name. Impress it on your heart. Write Him on your forehead every evening by making the sign of the cross and saying the words: Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews. This will preserve you from a sudden death.

St. Bernard of Clairvaux at prayer

St. Bernard of Clairvaux (1090–1153) writes to his monks: Whatever I always have on my lips, whatever I always have in my heart, whatever I write without ceasing, what constitutes my philosophy in its innermost core, is Jesus, and indeed Jesus as the Crucified.

In his commentary on the Song of Songs he describes the similarity of oil with the name of Jesus, which rests equally on three properties: Oil keeps the fire burning, nourishes the body and soothes pain. It is thus light, food and medicine. Now follow the same effects in the name of the Bridegroom: When He is preached, He shines; when He is contemplated, He nourishes; when He is invoked, He anoints and soothes. The Lord says: Call upon Me in the day of trouble, and I will deliver you. (Ps 49,15) […] Here, my soul, you have kept in the name of Jesus, as in a little flask, your elixir. It is absolutely healing and will never fail to take effect in all your illnesses. O carry this medicine always on your breast, keep it always at hand, so that all your thinking and acting may aim at Jesus.

St. Francis

Of the great love of St. Francis (1181–1226) for the Most Holy Name of Jesus, Thomas of Celano writes as if absorbed in prayer: More than can be humanly understood, he was inwardly moved whenever he spoke Your name, O holy Lord. Then he was full of rejoicing and of the most chaste joy, and he appeared truly as a new man and from another world. […]

Celano also describes how St. Francis continually contemplated the Incarnation and the suffering of Jesus: Above all it was the humility of the Incarnation of Jesus and the love shown in His Passion that so occupied his thoughts that he scarcely wished to think of anything else. […] When he spoke the name Child of Bethlehem or Jesus, he licked his lips with his tongue, as it were, relishing and savoring with his blessed palate the sweetness of this name.

St. Bernardine of Siena

Two hundred years later a spiritual son of St. Francis, St. Bernardine of Siena (1380–1440), became the standard-bearer of the name of Jesus. He never held a parish mission without a fervent sermon on the name of Jesus, whose veneration he recognized as the great remedy against the falling away from the faith. In his biography it says: When he finally saw that the glorious name, which is above every name, (Phil 2,9) had almost fallen into oblivion in the hearts of men, he was seized with the desire, as a true imitator of the Apostle, to bear this name before all kings and tribes and peoples. (Cf. Acts 9,15)

He sought to obtain a small wooden board on which this name was painted or drawn in golden letters. This board he held up before the crowds of people who gathered daily for his sermon. With a raised voice he proclaimed to them: This is your badge, this is the weapon of the People of God! This is the name, dearly beloved faithful, and there is no other in which you can be saved. (Cf. Acts 4,12) This is further the healing and loving name, whose image you should wear indelibly on your breast and look at there daily, and display in the public squares, and which each of you should paint visibly and in dignified beauty over the lintel of his house.

This is the name which, according to the teaching of the Apostle, you must keep before your eyes in all days of happiness and call upon in the days of misfortune. For this holy name is written, first, in the Book of Life, as the Prophet attests; then, on the inscription of the Cross of our Redemption, as we see from the Gospel, (cf. Jn 19,19) and further, it is written on the forehead of every saint in the eternal homeland.2 (Cf. Rev 22,4)

For the triumph and glorification of the victorious name of Jesus, for the salvation of souls and in reparation for the offenses against the Most Holy Name of Jesus, St. Bernardine earnestly recommended three very concrete forms of veneration to the people:

  1. The unceasing prayer of the heart through the invocation of the Most Holy Name of Jesus
  2. The monogram of the Name of Jesus as an outwardly visible sign
  3. The zealous spreading of devotion to the Most Holy Name of Jesus

Living in the Sun of the Most Holy Name of Jesus

The Key of the Son of David

The name Jesus is the shortest prayer of the heart and the key to enter the mysterious castle of our soul, which is created through and toward Jesus. (Cf. Col 1,12)

Jesus, who has opened heaven for us as Redeemer, also possesses the key to our soul. Only through Jesus can we truly recognize how God has thought of us and what calling He has placed in our heart for time and eternity. The name of Jesus is, as it were, the password to our own soul, but also the key to recognizing and understanding our neighbor rightly. In this sense a monk of the Eastern Church explains to us: We should approach all men and women – in the street, the shop, the office, the factory, the bus, wherever we stand in line, but especially those who seem unpleasant to us – with the name of Jesus in our heart and on our lips. Over them all we should pronounce His name, for their true name is the name Jesus. In them adore Christ, in them serve Christ. In many of these men and women Jesus is imprisoned. Set Him free by recognizing and adoring Him in them. When we go through the world with this new view, pronouncing Jesus over every person, they will be transformed and transfigured before us. The more we give ourselves in service to people, the more clear and alive the new view will become in us.3

And should we have the painful experience of being hurt or offended by our fellow men, the words of the Russian Pilgrim help us: When someone insults me, I only think how sweet the Jesus Prayer is; at once the offense and all anger have vanished, and I have forgotten everything.4

3 Im Namen Jesu ist Heil, by a monk of the Eastern Church, translated by Dr. Oswald Loretz, Innsbruck 1959, pp. 58–59, Imprimatur of the Apostolic Administration Innsbruck No. 2557/2 of December 31, 1958.

4 The Jesus Prayer helps us to recognize Christ in all people and to see all people in Christ. Therefore the Jesus Prayer has nothing to do with flight from the world or denial of the world. On the contrary, it is a form of extreme affirmation of life. The Jesus Prayer contains no rejection of God’s creation; rather, it shows the highest esteem for all people and things in God. The name of Jesus can become a mystical key to this world, an instrument of the hidden offering of all things and people; for He impresses the divine seal upon the world. The Adoration of the Name of Jesus (El Greco)

The Invocation of the Most Holy Name of Jesus

Bishop Johann Michael Sailer writes about the invocation of the Most Holy Name of Jesus: Every one who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved. (Rom 10,13) This is the promise of all promises, because it excludes no one who calls upon the name of the Lord, and promises to everyone the highest good. […] This promise does not let us expect some small gift, but the highest good: whoever calls upon the name of the Lord will be blessed. Blessedness, the highest good of man, comes from the Lord, who is rich for all.

If the one Lord, who is rich for all, is to give, then the needy must surely accept what is offered to them. Whoever does not recognize his sins, his misery, his poverty, or does not want to give honor to the name of the Lord and allow himself to become rich through Him, does not call upon the Lord as his Lord; he therefore despises the gift of His hands, remains poor and wretched, remains a sinner and keeps the curse of sin, remains without love and without blessedness. But whoever recognizes his sin and his poverty in love and blessedness, who in Christ recognizes his Lord, who is rich for all, will surely also say to Him: You who are rich for all, be so for me too! Teach me to love as You have loved. Be also my salvation and make me blessed in love! He speaks thus and receives one gift after another, and can love his Lord and in Him be blessed in love.

When we pronounce the name Jesus with reverence and love, we unite ourselves with our Redeemer and Savior, who, through the invocation of His Most Holy Name, is at once present Himself and touches and enriches our soul with His grace.5 For when we call upon His name, we also at the same time call down upon us His redemptive merits, which become powerful anew through this act of turning to our Lord and God.

When we once become truly aware of this, then this blessed truth will shine through our whole heart, and the royal way of invoking the Most Holy Name of Jesus will become the pulse of our life.

5 The power and glory of God are present and effective in His name. The name of God is the Godhead present: “Immanuel – God with us.” At the moment when one attentively and thoughtfully calls upon the name of God, one places oneself in His presence, opens oneself to His power and surrenders oneself as an instrument and offering into His hands. Such contact gives us a greater measure of supernatural life and of love of neighbor. Therefore the invocation of the Most Holy Name of Jesus has a very special sacramental character. (Ibid., p. 134)

The Last Supper

Our soul is an upper room in which the Last Supper can be celebrated at all times. Secretly He says to us as once before: I have earnestly desired to eat this passover with you. (Lk 22,15)

In the visible Eucharist Jesus is offered under the forms of bread and wine. In the Eucharist within us He can be designated and named through His Most Holy Name alone. Therefore we can make the invocation of the Most Holy Name a spiritual communion.6

6 Ibid., p. 67.

The repetition of the name of Jesus is comparable to the beat of the wings of a bird that rises into the air. It must never be strained and forced, hasty or noisy. It must be delicate, light, and – in the deepest sense of the word – graceful. When the bird has reached the desired height, it glides on in its flight, and only from time to time does it beat its wings in order to stay aloft. Thus the soul too, which has taken the thought of Jesus into itself and is filled with the “thinking-of-Him,” can let go of the repetition of the name and rest in our Lord. When other thoughts threaten to crowd out the thought of Jesus, then begin the invocation anew, in order to gain fresh uplift.7

7 Im Namen Jesu ist Heil, p. 26.

Peace and Reconciliation in the Most Holy Name of Jesus

In what way do we attain peace and reconciliation through the name of Jesus? Let us once more let our monk of the Eastern Church speak:

The name itself is a means of purification and perfection. A touchstone and a filter through which our thoughts, words and deeds must pass in order to be freed from their impurities. […] When we are tempted, the name of Jesus brings us victory and peace. A heart that is already filled with the name of Jesus and the presence of our Lord does not linger in any sinful thought or image. But we are weak, and often our defense collapses, and then temptation is like a roaring torrent. In such a case do not look at the temptation, do not wrestle with your own desire, do not look at the storm, and do not look at yourself. Look at our Lord, hold on to Him, invoke His Most Holy Name. When Peter walked toward Jesus over the water and saw the storm, he became afraid (Mt 14,30) and began to sink. The Hidden Treasure

If we could call all the heroic acts of the elect, all the sufferings of the martyrs, the virtues and merits of all the saints who have ever lived and who will live until the end of time, yes, even the virtues and merits of the Most Blessed Virgin herself our own, all of that would be only like a grain of sand in comparison with a single offering of Jesus!

Happy, a thousand times happy, is the soul that has found Jesus and knows how to make use of Him! With Jesus it has everything. He suffices for it.

This certainly does not mean that the veneration of the saints is useless; for when Christ crowns their merits, He crowns only His own gifts. And when we offer these same merits to God, we offer to Him only the merits of His own Son.

Let us therefore rejoice to offer to God the Father what is “of Jesus in the saints”; for that is what is so pleasing to the Father in them and what will form their eternal glorification. The most blessed Virgin Mary holds a special place in the hierarchy of the saints, because she was more inseparably united with Jesus than all others. When we say: Mary! she says: Jesus! When we go to Mary, she leads us to Jesus. Mary looked constantly at Jesus, united herself with Jesus, offered herself to Jesus. Jesus was everything to her, the whole content of her life. Mary will also teach us to make Jesus our “everything,” if we ask her for it.

But what does it mean, then, to offer Jesus, to make use of Him? How is it that in many devotional books this thought is hardly touched upon?

To offer Jesus! That means to present Him to His Father in reparation, with the words: Behold, I am nothing but weakness and misery; but I offer You the strength, the holiness, the purity, the virtues of Jesus. I am cold, but if I had all the love of the Seraphim, what could I give You that would be worthy of You? Heavenly Father, in place of my coldness I offer You the love of Jesus. I have just fallen, I have erred: I offer You the suffering and the Blood of Jesus for my cleansing and in reparation. Such evil impulses force themselves upon me against my will; for them I offer You the opposite acts of Jesus.

In this way even our weaknesses will serve to enrich us, since they give us the opportunity to make this infinite offering, which we perhaps would not have thought of if no evil had come our way.

To offer Jesus also means to offer Him as the Sanctifier of the good. Through this offering, those who not only unite their smallest actions with the whole conduct of Jesus, but virtually fuse them with it, receive back their small coins transformed into gold pieces.

If, for example, an act of obedience is required of us, we use our acts of obedience as an opportunity to offer to the Eternal Father the individual acts of obedience of Jesus, and we direct our whole attention to carrying them out with all possible perfection and faithfulness, in order thereby to remind Him again of His divine pleasure. If an occasion for humiliation presents itself, we say: Heavenly Father, I offer You the humiliations of Jesus. In suffering we do not look at ourselves, but offer the sufferings of Jesus.

How rich we would become if we understood how to unite ourselves with Jesus in everything in this way and to offer Him to the Heavenly Father! Let us above all make the offering of Jesus at Holy Communion. For He gives Himself to us only in order to enrich us and to enable us to return Him to His Father and offer Him, and through this offering to obtain for ourselves two infinite goods: to be able to bring God a gift that is worthy of Him, and thereby to draw down upon us a look of His love and of His divine pleasure.

Before Holy Communion we offer the Eternal Father the suffering of Jesus for the cleansing of our soul and in reparation for all our faithlessness. And after Holy Communion we want to offer Jesus again as a treasure that one fears to stain and that one wants to hand over unharmed to the one for whom it is destined.

The soul that has found Jesus in this way has found peace, and the more wretched it sees itself, the more it rejoices in this; for thus it contributes more and more to the eternal glorification of the infinite mercy of God.15

What Are You Doing?

Before Mary appeared at Fatima in 1917, the three seer children Lucia, Francisco and Jacinta were prepared for this great grace in 1916 by three visits of an angel of the Lord. At his second appearance the angel spoke the striking words to the children, who were absorbed in play:

“What are you doing?” This “What are you doing?” from the mouth of the angel is extraordinarily profound and contains for each of us a powerful message and a decisive question:

What are we doing with our talent for life? Do we squander this gift in the sandbox of transitoriness, or do we play before God the true song of love, like Mary, the Mother of God, of whom it is said:

This is a word that spans like a rainbow high over the heaven of our concepts, and only a spirit that can fly high and far will recognize this word clearly. Under “playing” we humans can understand, in a figurative sense, the delight of God, God’s rapture in Mary already through her being before God. She is like a crystal that shines a thousandfold and gives a different perspective on each facet, which lets angels and humans break out in jubilation and adoration of God.

And if we want to bring this word down to earth and understand it rightly, we can say that what was a “playing” for Mary, the sinless one, costs us human beings real effort and would have to be rendered for us as: “It is our delight, day by day, to be before God in adoration and in tireless zeal for everything good, God-willed and leading to God!” If we want always to think only of God, always want to delight Him through a small proof of love – in the form of a joyfully borne sacrifice, in the form of a new gift – and always return to Him like the ball into the child’s hand, then our life too is a loving being before God, a playing of the child at the hand of Mother Mary. What is our doing here on the small earth, measured against eternity, does it not look like playing?

But God takes this our “playing” very seriously. He has given us an angel at every step and every turn, so that our foot may not stumble, and he “plays” before God, he plays the song of our life, of our love for God; he sings for us the Gloria and the Credo and the Sanctus, the Agnus Dei and the Domine non sum dignus; he plays our jubilation on the harp. Every trait of our nature is a string. When it sounds for God, our angel carries our act of will, transformed into a tone, before our Lord like a song: our love, our striving, our obedience, our readiness, our saying yes, our silence and forgiving.
